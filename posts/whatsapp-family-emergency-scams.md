# WhatsApp Family Emergency Scams — 'I Have a New Number'

*The message reads exactly like something your child would write. The same words, the same pet name for you, even the small spelling mistake. You have half a minute to decide whether to send $800 to a stranger.*

---

> **The one rule that stops this scam:** Before you send any money, phone your family member on their old number — the one already saved in your phone. If they do not answer, wait. A real emergency can wait ten minutes for you to call back.

---

> **In short:** In the UK alone, families lost more than £1.5 million to this scam in one year — and the real figure is higher, because most people are too embarrassed to report it. The scam is simple: a number you do not know says it is your child with a new phone, makes you panic, and asks for money right away. The safe move is always the same — stop, and phone the old number first.

---

## The Message They Send

It is almost the same words in every family:

> *"Mum / Dad, it's me. My phone broke and I'm using a friend's phone. Please save this new number. I need to pay a bill urgently — can you send me the money? I'll explain when I see you. Don't call the old number, it isn't working."*

The whole message is built to stop you from doing the one thing that catches them: phoning the old number. Notice how it tells you not to.

Sometimes the scammer knows your family's names from social media or an old data leak. They may call you "Mumma ji" or "Papa ji," or use your child's real pet name for you. That does not mean it is your child.

---

## Six Warning Signs

**1. They pretend to be family.** A number you do not know says it is your son or daughter. WhatsApp does not check who owns a number — anyone can make a new number and write anything.

**2. They play on your love.** "I'm embarrassed to ask, but I have no one else." "I'm in trouble and I need you." The message is built to switch on a parent's instinct to help right away.

**3. They rush you.** "It has to be today." "I need it in the next hour." A real family emergency can wait for a two-minute phone call. If it cannot wait for you to call back — it is not real.

**4. They offer a reward or make a threat.** "I'll pay you back double." Or "If you don't help me now, something bad will happen." Both are tricks.

**5. They tell you not to check.** "Don't call the old number — it's broken." Telling you not to check is the clearest sign that the person is not who they say they are.

**6. They ask for money in a way you can't undo.** Bank transfer, Zelle, Venmo, Western Union, or cryptocurrency. Real family understand if you want to check first.

---

## A Family Safe Word

Set one up with your family tonight. Pick a word that only your family knows — something a stranger could not guess from social media. The name of your first pet. Your mother's village. A made-up word you only use at home.

> **The rule:** If anyone — even someone who sounds exactly like your child — asks for money and cannot say the safe word, do not send anything. Phone the old number. If no answer, phone another family member. Wait.

---

## What to Do

**If you get this message:**

1. Do not reply yet. Do not send money.
2. Phone your family member on their old number — the one already in your phone.
3. If no answer: phone another family member, or wait ten to fifteen minutes and try again.
4. If the old number answers and it is your child — good. The "new number" message was a scam.
5. Block and report the unknown number on WhatsApp.

**If you already sent money:**

1. Phone your bank right away — within minutes if you can — and ask them to try to stop it.
2. Report it (see the list below).
3. Take a screenshot of the WhatsApp messages before you block the number.
4. Do not feel ashamed. This scam is built to trick loving, careful people. It is not your fault.

## Where to Get Help and Report

If you are not sure whether a message is a scam, you can phone a free helpline in your country. Real people will talk it through with you — you do not have to decide alone.

| Country   | Free helpline — call to ask or get help        | Where to report                                                                    |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| USA       | AARP Fraud Watch Network — **877-908-3360**    | FBI IC3 [ic3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov) · FTC [reportfraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov) |
| Canada    | Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre — **1-888-495-8501** | [antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca](https://antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca) |
| UK        | Citizens Advice consumer helpline — **0808 223 1133** | Action Fraud **0300 123 2040** · [reportfraud.police.uk](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk) |
| Australia | IDCARE — **1800 595 160** (also New Zealand)   | Scamwatch [scamwatch.gov.au](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au)                          |

You do not have to be a member of any of these to call. The helplines are free. On WhatsApp, you can also report a number: open the chat → tap the name → Report.
