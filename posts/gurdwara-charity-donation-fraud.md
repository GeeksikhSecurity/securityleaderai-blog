# Gurdwara & Charity Donation Fraud — Protecting the Seva Ecosystem

*Vand Chhakna — sharing with others — sits at the heart of Sikh life. Scammers know this, and they build their fake fundraisers around it.*

---

> **The one check that stops this scam:** Before you give to any fundraiser, phone the gurdwara yourself — using the number from Google Maps or their own website, not the number in the fundraiser. Ask if the fundraiser is really theirs. If they do not know about it, it is fake.

---

> **In short:** Fake charity fundraisers cost families billions every year. Our community is targeted on purpose, because we are known for giving generously. The fakes look real — a langar fundraiser, a Punjab flood relief drive, a caller who speaks Panjabi — but the gurdwara has never heard of them. The safe move is always the same: phone the gurdwara first, and give to them directly.

---

## Why Our Community Is Targeted

Vand Chhakna — sharing what you earn with those in need — means our community gives more freely than almost any other. That is something to be proud of. But scammers study this too. They know a fake "langar emergency" sent to a WhatsApp group of 200 Panjabi families will get more money than a plain ask.

This is not a fault in our values. Vand Chhakna does not change. The aim is simply to put our seva in the right hands — not into the pockets of people who exploit it.

---

## The Common Tricks

**Fake gurdwara fundraiser.** A GoFundMe or money request goes around family WhatsApp groups, claiming to be for a local gurdwara's langar, a new building, or a youth program. It may use photos taken from the gurdwara's own social media. The gurdwara committee has never heard of it.

**Punjab disaster relief.** After a real flood or tragedy in Punjab, fake "relief fund" links appear within hours. They copy news photos. Some are made before the real charities have even started collecting.

**Caller pretending to be a gurdwara.** Someone phones claiming to collect for a "Sikh Welfare Foundation" or "Punjab Relief Committee" — names that may not be real. They ask for cash, gift cards, or a transfer. The number on your phone may look local. They may speak Panjabi well.

**Copied real fundraiser.** Scammers copy a real, honest campaign's photos and words, make a near-identical one on another site, and spread the fake first. People think they are giving to the real cause.

---

## Six Warning Signs

**1. They use a trusted name without permission.** A fundraiser claims to be tied to a named gurdwara, a Sikh organization, or a known community leader who has never heard of it. Photos of Harmandir Sahib or a local gurdwara are often used to look real.

**2. They play on your feelings and faith.** "Thousands of hungry children." "Langar will close without you." "Waheguru will bless those who give." Fake fundraisers use our own values to rush us into giving.

**3. They rush you.** "The fund closes in 48 hours." "Give now to match the donation." The rush is there to stop you from phoning the gurdwara to check.

**4. They offer a blessing or a reward.** "An anonymous donor will double your gift." "Waheguru sees your seva." Fake fundraisers wrap the spiritual meaning of giving around the trick.

**5. The details do not add up.** The organizer's name is in no gurdwara record. The money goes to a personal PayPal or Venmo, not a registered charity or the gurdwara's own account. No charity number is given.

**6. They ask for an odd way to pay.** Gift cards, transfers, or cryptocurrency for a charity is a clear warning sign. Real gurdwaras and charities take cheques, cards, or proper donation sites that give a tax receipt.

---

## How to Check Before You Give

**Step 1 — Phone the gurdwara yourself.** Find the gurdwara's number on Google Maps or their own website — not from the fundraiser. Phone and ask: *"Is there really a fundraiser running for this?"* If they have never heard of it, it is not theirs.

**Step 2 — Check that the charity is registered.**

| Country   | Tool                   | Website                                                                            |
| --------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| USA       | IRS Tax Exempt Search  | [apps.irs.gov/app/eos](https://apps.irs.gov/app/eos)                               |
| Canada    | CRA Charity Search     | [canada.ca/en/services/taxes/charities](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/charities.html) |
| UK        | Charity Commission     | [register-of-charities.charitycommission.gov.uk](https://register-of-charities.charitycommission.gov.uk) |
| Australia | ACNC                   | [acnc.gov.au/charity](https://www.acnc.gov.au/charity)                             |
| India     | NGO Darpan / 80G       | [ngodarpan.gov.in](https://ngodarpan.gov.in)                                       |

A real charity in your country should be listed. No registration means no tax receipt — so check before you give.

**Step 3 — Give to the gurdwara directly.** If the cause is real, give at the gurdwara itself or through their confirmed bank details — not through a link you found on social media.

---

## A Note on Seva

Checking a charity before you give is not a lack of faith or generosity. It is Kirat Karni — giving your effort and money with care and intent. Giving without checking robs both you and the real cause you meant to help. The pause to check is itself an act of seva.

---

## If You Have Already Given to a Fake

If you have already sent money, you can phone a free helpline in your country. Real people will help you work out what to do next.

| Country   | Free helpline — call to ask or get help        | Where to report                                                                    |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| USA       | AARP Fraud Watch Network — **877-908-3360**    | FBI IC3 [ic3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov) · FTC [reportfraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov) |
| Canada    | Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre — **1-888-495-8501** | [antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca](https://antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca) |
| UK        | Citizens Advice consumer helpline — **0808 223 1133** | Action Fraud **0300 123 2040** · [reportfraud.police.uk](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk) |
| Australia | IDCARE — **1800 595 160** (also New Zealand)   | Scamwatch [scamwatch.gov.au](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au)                          |

You do not have to be a member to call. The helplines are free. Phone your bank first if money has gone — they may be able to stop it.
